# The Safest College Degrees From AI Disruption (Forbes)

**Summary**: A Forbes contributor piece (August 2026) naming 15 college majors considered relatively AI-resistant — nursing, computer science, accounting, civil/mechanical/electrical engineering, physical therapy, cybersecurity, data science, K-12 education, finance, healthcare administration, HR management, construction management, and social work — built on BLS growth projections and an "AI-Resistant Careers Index," alongside a durable-skills list nearly identical to the wiki's existing transferable-skills framework.

**Sources**: `raw/misc/The College Degrees Considered Safest From AI Disruption.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-23

## About this source

Forbes contributor piece by Sarah Hernholm (August 17, 2026), aimed at students and parents choosing majors. It draws on BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook projections through 2034 and "The AI-Resistant Careers Index 2026" (a separate Forbes ranking). This is a popular-press, decision-support artifact rather than original research — its value to the wiki is in the specific new statistic it surfaces and in how closely its conclusions converge, independently, with the wiki's existing academic and labor-economics evidence base.

## The headline statistic

**Recent-graduate unemployment reached 5.6% in Q2 2026** (Federal Reserve Bank of New York), surpassing the overall workforce rate of 4.2% — a **reversal of the historical norm** in which a college degree reliably conferred more job security than the general labor market. This is a new, citable, precisely-dated figure for the wiki's broken-ladder evidence thread — more specific than the "8-11 percentage point decline in junior-hire share" language already in [[broken-ladder]], and a good complement to the "80% drop in entry-level hiring" and "9 million jobs displaced by 2030" figures the article also cites from Forbes' own reporting and the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025.

## What makes a degree "safe" — the article's framework

The piece is explicit that **no degree is fully AI-proof**, including computer science, "long considered a safe bet." A degree holds up when the underlying work combines: judgment, hands-on skill, or in-person trust; steady projected demand; and manageable competition. This is functionally identical to the wiki's existing [[applied-co-intelligence-model]] framing (technical skill × transferable skill × AI mastery within occupational context) and the [[ai-occupational-exposure-index]]'s finding that AI correlates most strongly with cognitive tasks and least with physical/judgment/trust-based ones.

## The 15 degrees named

Grouped by wiki-relevant CTE overlap:

**Direct CTE-pathway overlap** (all already represented in the wiki's CTE cluster-exposure evidence):
- **Nursing (BSN)** — median wage $93,600; 5% BLS growth through 2034; "an algorithm can flag an abnormal vital sign, but a nurse still has to interpret it"
- **Civil Engineering** — median $99,590; 5% growth; explicitly noted as outperforming *other* engineering specialties due to the construction/data-center boom and PE-license accountability
- **Mechanical Engineering** — median $102,320; 9% growth (faster than average); framed as safe *because of*, not despite, rising automation — someone has to design and maintain the machinery
- **Electrical Engineering** — median $127,590 (electronics engineers); 7% growth; tied to grid modernization and semiconductor demand
- **Physical Therapy** — median $105,450; 15% growth; "about as hands-on as healthcare gets"
- **Construction Management** — median $106,980; 9% growth; tied to a "persistent nationwide construction labor shortage"

**Knowledge-work fields flagged as newly vulnerable but still relatively protected**:
- **Computer Science** — median $133,080 (software developers); 15% growth, but explicit acknowledgment that "AI coding assistants have already changed how entry-level programming work gets done, and junior roles have felt the squeeze" — safest for students who specialize in security, systems, or AI/ML engineering
- **Accounting** — median $81,680; 5% growth; AI automates bookkeeping/reconciliation but cannot sign off on an audit or assume legal liability
- **Cybersecurity** — median $124,910 (information security analysts); **29% growth, the fastest projected rate on the list** — AI simultaneously enables more sophisticated attacks and increases defensive demand
- **Data Science** — median $112,590; **34% growth, the fastest on the list overall** — framed as the degree most directly tied to the AI boom itself (students build and monitor the models reshaping other fields)
- **Finance** — median $101,350; 6% growth; AI has automated junior-analyst spreadsheet work, but risk judgment and regulatory interpretation remain human
- **Healthcare Administration** — median $117,960; 23% growth; the business/operations side of a growing healthcare sector
- **Human Resources Management** — median $72,910; 6% growth

**Relational/mission-driven fields**:
- **K-12 Education** — median $62,340 (elementary teachers); roughly flat overall employment but ~103,800 openings/year from retirements; explicitly "relationship-driven work" AI cannot replace
- **Social Work** — median $61,330 (median $68,090 for healthcare social workers); 6% growth overall, **10% growth specifically for mental-health/substance-abuse social work**

## The skills list — near-identical convergence with the wiki's transferable-skills framework

The article's nine "skills that increase job safety" — AI/data literacy, critical thinking, communication, emotional intelligence, adaptability, project/time management, deep domain expertise, collaboration, and ethical judgment — map almost one-to-one onto the wiki's existing [[transferable-skills]] and [[ai-mastery-continuum]] pages. Two points worth flagging as independent corroboration rather than duplication:

- It cites NACE's Job Outlook 2025 survey (237 companies): **nearly 90% name critical thinking as essential**, while employers report graduates arrive underprepared in it — an independent employer-survey confirmation of the same gap [[berwick-employers-ai-education-gap]] documents from IBM's and career consultants' perspectives ("prompt engineering is the floor, not the ceiling").
- It explicitly states these skills are buildable outside the classroom (internships, part-time work, volunteer leadership) — directly reinforcing the [[applied-co-intelligence-model]]'s insistence that AI mastery and transferable skills must be developed *in occupational context*, not as standalone coursework.

## Implications for CTE

1. **This is the first source in the wiki to translate labor-market exposure findings directly into a student/parent-facing "major selection" framework** — a bridge between the wiki's academic evidence base ([[ai-occupational-exposure-index]], [[canaries-coal-mine-brynjolfsson]]) and the practical decision CTE counselors and career-pathway advisors actually need to support.
2. **Six of the fifteen "safe" degrees are direct CTE-pipeline occupations** (nursing, civil/mechanical/electrical engineering, construction management, and — with a 2-year/technical-credential pathway rather than a 4-year degree in many states — cybersecurity), giving CTE program marketing a specific, externally validated (BLS-sourced) list to point to.
3. **The reversal of the college-wage-premium norm (5.6% recent-grad unemployment vs. 4.2% overall) is a concrete new statistic for the CTE-vs-four-year-degree argument** already present in [[nyt-teens-trade-classes]] and [[broken-ladder]] — it corroborates the College ROI Collapse narrative documented in student interviews with a specific, dated federal statistic.
4. **The article's own caution — "no degree is completely safe... not even computer science" — is a useful counter to any CTE messaging that oversells trade/technical pathways as automation-proof.** The safest framing, consistent with the wiki's [[applied-co-intelligence-model]], is judgment/trust/hands-on-skill plus continuous AI-literacy upskilling, not any single credential.
