# Work-Based Learning

**Summary**: Concept page for work-based learning (WBL): internships, apprenticeships, co-ops, and employer-embedded training. Across the wiki's labor-market evidence it has become the most frequently proposed answer to AI eroding the entry-level "training ground." This page gathers what the wiki's sources say about why WBL matters more in the AI era, the models in the evidence base, the design problem AI creates *inside* WBL, and how thin the evidence on WBL's effectiveness still is.

## What it is

Work-based learning covers structured learning that takes place in, or is directly tied to, a real workplace: internships, registered and youth apprenticeships, co-ops, clinical placements, and employer-run training programs. It sits at the "preparation" end of what the Alliance for Learning Innovation calls the **career-connected learning continuum**: career awareness from middle school, then exploration through job shadowing and internships, then preparation through CTE and work-based learning (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).

## Why AI raises the stakes: the disappearing training ground

The wiki's labor-market sources converge on a mechanism in which AI weakens the entry-level jobs through which people have always built expertise:

- **The "Flipped Pyramid."** In "Growth Roles," AI performs the junior work that used to be the learning path (research, first drafts, basic analysis), so firms need fewer juniors and more experienced staff. "Companies still need human expertise. They just don't have as much need for the entry-level roles where people have traditionally developed that expertise" (source: No+Country+for+Young+Grads+V_Final7.29.25+(1).pdf).
- **The expertise gap, from the employer side.** ABB's Guillaume Delacour notes it can take 10–15 years to build high-level expertise, yet many of the foundational early-career tasks that built it can now be done largely by AI (source: CEMS AI Report_ Augmented leadership_2026.pdf).
- **Firms tilt toward seniors.** At AI-adopting firms across 41 countries, the junior share of employment falls 1.9 points, mainly because senior employment grows 6.7% (source: HowDoesAIChangeLaborDemand.pdf). The authors warn this "may have implications for the pipeline of experienced workers that firms will draw on in the future," and note policy calls to "build other routes into skilled work" (source: HowDoesAIChangeLaborDemand.pdf).
- **Tacit knowledge is the durable asset.** AI substitutes for codified "book-learning" knowledge more readily than for tacit, experience-built judgment, and the tacit side of that finding is the more robust one (source: Canaries_August2026.pdf). Tacit knowledge is what workplaces, not classrooms, teach.
- **Grades are losing signal value.** If AI inflates grades and work samples, employers struggle to read skill from them (source: Gradiating into Disruption.pdf). Supervised performance in a real workplace is harder to fake.

**The implication the wiki draws**: if firms stop training juniors, someone has to. WBL is the main candidate to replace the disappearing junior rung (see [[entry-level-labor-market]], [[no-country-young-grads-burning-glass]], [[ai-labor-demand-41-countries]]). This is the wiki's inference from these sources, not a finding any single study tested.

## Models in the wiki's evidence base

| Model | What it does | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Alabama Data Scholars** (QuantHub + Alabama Dept. of Education) | Paid AI/data internships for high-school students (100+ placements across 50+ employers); industry credentials before graduation; curriculum refreshed every semester (source: Senate Hearing on Artificial Intelligence and K-12 Education.md). The 2026 cohort placed 52 interns at 23 organizations, with curriculum mapped to 43 real internship roles (source: Preparing Students for AI & Data Careers.md) | [[quanthub-data-scholars-2026-update]], [[senate-hearing-ai-k12-education]] |
| **Meta America's Workforce Academy** | $115M of free training for data-center construction and operations with NCCER credentials; its Level-Up predecessor drew 35,000 applications in its first week (source: Mark Zuckerberg Says America Needs 'Hundreds Of Thousands' Of Skilled Tradespeople As Meta Launches Workforce Academy To Provide 'Free Training'.md) | [[meta-workforce-academy]] |
| **Employer-led AI training** | IBM SkillsBuild, the Google/Camden London AI Campus; AI certificates alone are a "hygiene factor," not a differentiator (source: Employers step in to fill the AI education gap.md) | [[berwick-employers-ai-education-gap]] |
| **"Sim-ternships"** | The ACI report asks employers to fund AI-enabled work-based learning and co-design curriculum (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf) | [[applied-co-intelligence-report]] |
| **Direct-from-high-school hiring** | Deloitte and Shopify reported hiring from high school and training in-house (a practitioner observation, not research) (source: New Book Podcast_ AI Unplugged_ The Hype and Hope in Education Futures.md) | [[ai-unplugged-murgatroyd]] |

Policy sources consistently back WBL. The Fostering AI-Ready reports urge policymakers to fund apprenticeships: the Building & Moving report adds work-based learning explicitly (source: Fostering-AI-Ready-Building-and-Moving-CTE-Pipelines.pdf), and the Cultivating Resources and Caring for Communities reports make the same call (source: Fostering-AI-Ready-Cultivating-Resources-CTE-Pipelines.pdf; Fostering-AI-Ready-Caring-for-Communities-CTE-Pipelines.pdf). See [[fostering-ai-ready-building-moving]]. Advance CTE's strategy 3 calls for continuous industry collaboration and ongoing employer validation of AI-related skills (source: Responsive_By_Design_August_26_FINAL.pdf; see [[responsive-by-design-career-clusters]]).

## The design problem AI creates *inside* WBL

WBL does not escape the mechanism it is meant to answer. If AI automates the low-stakes, repetitive tasks that traditionally build competence, whether junior analyst work or entry-level trade tasks, then apprenticeships built on "learn by doing the easy stuff first" face the same problem as corporate junior roles (source: CEMS AI Report_ Augmented leadership_2026.pdf; see [[cems-augmented-leadership]]). An internship where the intern supervises AI output without ever doing the underlying work may reproduce the learning-performance paradox ([[building-ai-companions]], [[agentivism]]) in a workplace setting.

This suggests, as an inference from the wiki's learning-science pages rather than a tested finding, that AI-era WBL should:
- **sequence the work**: have learners do core tasks unaided before delegating them to AI, mirroring the "Brain-to-LLM" finding in [[your-brain-on-chatgpt]];
- **build in verification**: make checking AI output part of the job, as in the [[applied-co-intelligence-model]];
- **assess performance directly**: rely on supervisor-observed performance and demonstrations rather than work products that AI could have produced.

## The evidence problem

The wiki recommends WBL often, but its effectiveness is poorly measured:
- **No common definition.** A Bellwether/Britebound review of 10 states found each defines what counts as WBL differently, collects different data, and tracks it with different infrastructure. "Even in states collecting data, few can say which approaches move outcomes, for which students, and under what conditions" (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).
- **Earnings can't be measured.** The strongest CTE outcome study in the wiki (Nebraska/South Dakota) found higher on-time graduation (+7 points) and postsecondary enrollment (+10 points) among pathway completers, but could not measure earnings because education and workforce records aren't linked (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md). That study covered structured CTE pathways, not WBL specifically.
- **No causal WBL evidence in the wiki.** None of the models above has a comparison-group evaluation in the wiki's sources. Alabama Data Scholars and Meta's academy are documented through program descriptions and company announcements, and Meta's own data show AI infrastructure creates far more temporary construction jobs than permanent ones (about 1,800 at peak versus about 100 operational roles in Texas) (source: Mark Zuckerberg Says America Needs 'Hundreds Of Thousands' Of Skilled Tradespeople As Meta Launches Workforce Academy To Provide 'Free Training'.md).

The case for WBL in the AI era therefore rests on a strong *mechanism*: entry-level training is eroding, and workplaces teach tacit skills. It does not yet rest on strong *outcome* evidence. See [[career-pathways-evidence-gap]].

## Implications for CTE

1. **WBL is CTE's structural advantage, and the AI-era argument for it is strong.** CTE is the part of the education system already built around workplace learning, which the labor-market evidence increasingly suggests will matter most.
2. **Redesign placements for AI.** Programs should check whether AI is quietly removing the learning tasks inside their internships and apprenticeships, and sequence work so students build competence before they delegate.
3. **Measure it.** Programs and states adopting WBL should use common definitions and link outcomes to workforce data. Without that, WBL's expansion will repeat the pattern in [[career-pathways-evidence-gap]]: growth without evidence.
4. **Favor verifiable credentials.** Pair placements with industry credentials (NCCER, the Alabama model) and supervisor-assessed performance, the hard-to-fake signals that gain value as grades lose it.

## The Training-Ground Problem, Reported by an AI Company About Itself (October 2026)

[[employment-at-anthropic]] states the design problem above in the employer's own words: as Anthropic employees automate their tasks, in some roles "they are increasingly removed from the object-level work that originally built their skills" (source: How employment is changing at Anthropic.md). Its junior technical hiring share fell from 27% to 9%, and its redeployment practices, by the authors' own account, do nothing for people "not hired to begin with" (source: How employment is changing at Anthropic.md). Even at the most AI-intensive employer, the junior training rung is the part that disappears, which strengthens the case for building it elsewhere.

## "Experience Debt": The Employer-Side Name for the Problem (October 2026)

A Dayforce survey of nearly 7,000 respondents in six countries ([[experience-debt-dayforce]]) names the training-ground problem "experience debt": "the future capability cost created when people lose opportunities to learn by doing." 65% say their people are losing the ability to do tasks AI now does for them, and only 27% have enough work time to develop skills (source: Is your organisation at risk of 'experience debt' from AI_.md). If workplaces are losing learning-by-doing, structured WBL with protected development time becomes more important. The figures are self-reported perceptions from an HR software vendor.
