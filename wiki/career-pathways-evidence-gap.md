# Career Pathways Are Growing but Evidence Isn't

**Summary**: A Fast Company op-ed (August 2026) by the executive director of the Alliance for Learning Innovation, arguing that states are rapidly building career-connected learning pathways without the data infrastructure to know whether they work. It contributes the wiki's strongest CTE-specific outcome evidence: a Nebraska/South Dakota evaluation showing CTE pathway completers graduated and enrolled in postsecondary education at higher rates. It also documents why that study couldn't measure earnings, and proposes three federal fixes.

**Sources**: `raw/misc/Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md`, `raw/PDF/Gradiating into Disruption.pdf`

**Last updated**: 2026-10-28

---

## About this source

Fast Company op-ed by Sara Schapiro, executive director of the Alliance for Learning Innovation (ALI), published August 19, 2026 (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md). **This is an advocacy piece**: ALI is a coalition that lobbies for federal education R&D investment, and the recommendations come from ALI's own federal policy brief. The factual claims come from third-party studies it cites, which the wiki has not ingested directly.

---

## The problem: pathways outpacing evidence

- **Demand is moving fast.** Demand for AI skills in entry-level jobs "nearly tripled" since fall 2025, according to NACE. High-school seniors are graduating into that market, and states are building pathways "at a pace nobody would have predicted five years ago" (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).
- **Career-connected learning** runs as a continuum: career awareness from middle school, exploration through job shadowing and internships, and preparation through CTE and work-based learning (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).
- **States measure it inconsistently.** A Bellwether/Britebound report on work-based learning in 10 states found each uses different definitions of what counts, collects different data, and tracks it with different infrastructure. "Even in states collecting data, few can say which approaches move outcomes, for which students, and under what conditions" (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).

---

## The Nebraska case: evidence that moved policy, and hit a wall

A federally funded Regional Educational Laboratory (REL Central) evaluation found that students in Nebraska and South Dakota who **completed structured CTE pathways** were (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md):
- **7 percentage points more likely to graduate on time**, and
- **10 percentage points more likely to enroll in postsecondary education** within two years.

That evidence helped Nebraska lawmakers authorize the state's **first-ever dedicated CTE appropriation**, and CTE is now offered in every public high school in the state (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).

**The wall.** The researchers also set out to measure whether CTE students *earned more* after high school, but couldn't, "because there is no infrastructure linking education records to workforce data." In the author's words, one of the best evaluations "failed to answer the question that matters the most to students" (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).

*Caveat:* the op-ed doesn't describe the study's design. Pathway *completers* may differ from non-completers in ways that also raise graduation rates, so these figures should not be read as causal effects without checking the underlying REL report.

## Three federal recommendations (from ALI's policy brief)

1. **Start with data.** Build statewide longitudinal data systems that follow students from preschool through the workforce. Kentucky and Colorado already do this and use the results "to inform everything from policy to classroom instruction" (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).
2. **Modernize federal education R&D** (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md):
   - an ARPA-inspired Education and Workforce R&D Center with hiring flexibility and program-manager autonomy; or
   - a Career-Connected Learning Panel at the Institute of Education Sciences, modeled on the National Reading Panel, to review causal evidence and tell states which approaches to scale.
3. **Coordinate funding** across NSF and the Departments of Labor, Commerce, and Defense. The DOL/Commerce/BLS **AI Workforce Research Hub**, which evaluates how AI is reshaping work and turns findings into guidance for states and schools, is cited as the model to expand (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).

Rep. Suzanne Bonamici (D-Ore.) argued at ALI's June 2026 congressional briefing that students and teachers are "navigating a rapidly changing world without a map," and that the federal government has funded that kind of map for defense, health care, and energy but not education (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).

## Connections to the wiki

- **It is a concrete instance of the "evidence gap" obstacle.** The [[cte-futures-aci-webinar]] and [[applied-co-intelligence-report]] name the lack of longitudinal studies on CTE wage outcomes as one of four implementation obstacles. The Nebraska study is a real example of hitting that gap.
- **The Census paper shows what linkage makes possible.** [[graduating-into-disruption-census]] could follow 6.7 million graduates into the labor market only because its PSEO data link postsecondary records to employment records (source: Gradiating into Disruption.pdf). That is exactly the education-to-workforce linkage the Nebraska study lacked. The same kind of analysis for high-school CTE pathways would need the state data systems ALI calls for.
- **It parallels the K-12 AI evidence gap.** [[evidence-base-ai-k12]] and [[senate-hearing-ai-k12-education]] document a thin causal evidence base for AI in K-12; this source documents the same problem for career pathways.

## Implications for CTE

1. **CTE has a positive, policy-moving outcome finding.** Higher on-time graduation and postsecondary enrollment among pathway completers is useful evidence for CTE's value, with the causal caveat above.
2. **The earnings question is unanswered because of plumbing, not research design.** Programs cannot show wage returns without data systems linking K-12 records to workforce records. That gap matters more as AI reshapes which jobs pay ([[entry-level-labor-market]]).
3. **Work-based learning needs common definitions before it can be evaluated** (see [[work-based-learning]]). As the wiki increasingly points to work-based learning as a replacement for the disappearing junior rung ([[ai-labor-demand-41-countries]], [[entry-level-labor-market]]), the finding that 10 states define and track it 10 different ways is a practical barrier to knowing what works.
