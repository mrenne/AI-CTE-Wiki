## Key Themes Across the Wiki

- **Performance ≠ Learning**: AI-assisted task completion does not automatically produce durable understanding (see [[agentivism]], [[ai-tutoring]], [[khanmigo]])
- **Integration over isolation**: AI skills must be embedded in occupational context, not taught as standalone courses (see [[applied-co-intelligence-model]], [[ai-mastery-continuum]])
- **Human work persists**: Transferable skills — empathy, ethical reasoning, communication — remain the primary long-term differentiator (see [[transferable-skills]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **Equity is not automatic**: AI's benefits flow unevenly; without intentional design, AI diffusion exacerbates existing inequities (see [[cte-and-ai]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **Sycophancy is the enemy of both learning and safety**: AI's trained agreeableness undermines skill development and can amplify distorted beliefs in vulnerable users (see [[ai-sycophancy]], [[ai-delusional-spirals]], [[llms-workplace-social-skills]])
- **Design is the decisive variable**: Tutoring-specific AI (hints, guided questions) produces near-equivalent outcomes to textbooks; general-purpose AI (complete answers) harms independent learning and widens achievement gaps (see [[evidence-base-ai-k12]], [[ai-tutoring]], [[khanmigo]])
- **Educator AI outperforms student AI**: The most consistent positive causal evidence is for educator-facing tools — especially real-time coaching that benefits novice teachers most (see [[evidence-base-ai-k12]], [[ai-tutoring]])
- **Smaller can be better**: Domain-specific, locally-deployed SLMs may outperform general LLMs for institutional education use cases on cost, privacy, safety, and customization (see [[small-language-models]], [[ai-privacy-and-institutional-adoption]])
- **The equity inversion**: AI's first wave of labor market disruption is landing on educated, high-paid white-collar workers — not low-skill workers — while young workers face a hiring slowdown rather than mass unemployment (see [[anthropic-labor-market-index]], [[labor-market-polarization]], [[ai-economics-scan-2026]])
- **AI builds cognitive debt, not capability**: Repeated habitual AI reliance before building independent skills produces measurable neurological harm visible in EEG — the opposite of learning (see [[cognitive-debt]], [[your-brain-on-chatgpt]], [[agentivism]])
- **The relational economy is the CTE economy**: Structural change economics predicts that AI commodifying cognitive work will shift employment into care, hospitality, education, and artisanal production — the sectors CTE prepares (see [[relational-economy]], [[what-will-be-scarce]], [[cte-and-ai]])
- **Relational skills are becoming scarce at the moment they matter most**: Young people are spending less time with each other; AI may accelerate social withdrawal at the same moment human connection becomes economically central (see [[ai-job-apocalypse-klein]], [[cognitive-debt]], [[transferable-skills]])
- **AI tutoring barely works for high school students**: Meta-analytic evidence (34 studies) shows near-zero cognitive benefit of AI interventions for students in grades 10–12 — the core CTE population — while upper-primary students benefit substantially (see [[ai-agents-k12-meta-analysis]], [[cte-and-ai]])
- **The AI apocalypse narrative is itself a risk**: Galloway and Klein converge on the view that mass AI unemployment is unlikely (US tech employment flat, Jevons Paradox); but Shiller's narrative economics warns that widely believed fear stories can become self-fulfilling regardless of the underlying technology (see [[apocalypse-no-galloway]], [[ai-job-apocalypse-klein]])
- **Students cannot evaluate AI quality — and enthusiasm makes it worse**: Middle-schoolers using ChatGPT show chance-level ability to discriminate good from bad prompts or answers; positive AI attitudes negatively predict interaction quality; metacognition is the only protective factor identified (see [[illusion-of-understanding]], [[agentivism]], [[evidence-base-ai-k12]])
- **Only 12% of US jobs face true substitution**: BCG's six-segment analysis of 165M US jobs finds only 12% (Substituted) face genuine headcount contraction — far below the Frey & Osborne "47% at risk" figure — because demand expandability and augmentation dynamics protect the rest; CTE sectors constitute the core of the 57% insulated from displacement (see [[bcg-ai-reshapes-jobs]], [[ai-labor-disruption-segments]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **Human interaction and judgment is the decisive variable**: BCG's empirical framework operationalizes what makes a role augmented vs. substituted — roles with high human interaction intensity and low process structure land in protected segments; this gives transferable skills a concrete economic mechanism, not just a normative claim (see [[transferable-skills]], [[ai-labor-disruption-segments]], [[relational-economy]])
- **The K-12 AI policy gap is structural, not accidental**: 80% of students use AI for schoolwork; only 6% of teachers have clear school AI policies; no state has teacher training standards for AI; AP CS excludes AI content; state guidance is nonbinding — students are integrating AI without any institutional literacy framework (see [[stanford-ai-index-education-2026]], [[cte-and-ai]], [[ai-in-k12-education]])
- **AI infrastructure creates real trade jobs — with distributional costs**: Causal IV evidence from 3,100+ US counties confirms that data center construction raises local construction employment (+7.1%) and income (+8.2%), validating CTE infrastructure pipeline claims — but also raises electricity prices and house prices, with costs falling disproportionately on renters and low-income households (see [[nber-data-centers-local-economies]], [[cte-and-ai]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **AI bans fail; AI audits work**: The most practical secondary-level AI literacy framework in the wiki — students discover sycophancy, voice, and source bias firsthand by studying AI as a curriculum object; the AI audit (demonstrate you did not relinquish your thinking) operationalizes agentivism in a classroom (see [[middle-path-ai-literacy-nurenberg]], [[agentivism]], [[illusion-of-understanding]])
- **Work AI and learning AI are different engineering problems**: The learning-performance paradox (Khosravi et al.) names the structural mismatch: AI optimized for output quality reliably improves task performance while undermining the productive struggle that produces durable learning; prompt-level guardrails produce near-null effects — effective learning AI requires architectural redesign, not instructional wrapping (see [[building-ai-companions]], [[cognitive-debt]], [[agentivism]], [[khanmigo]])
- **Task unbundling, not job elimination, is the primary mechanism**: AI automates tasks within jobs, not jobs themselves — a financial analyst becomes a financial strategist as AI takes the procedural layer; skills replace job titles as the organizing unit of the workforce; the "independence era" of entrepreneurial contracting follows (see [[sinead-bovell-job-market]], [[labor-market-polarization]], [[bcg-ai-reshapes-jobs]])
- **Technology is not destiny — but deliberate action is required**: Acemoglu & Johnson's *Power and Progress* argument, surfaced in the CTE Futures webinar: AI's labor market effects depend on which institutions and stakeholders actively shape its deployment; without countervailing institutional action, default trajectories favor employers and high-skill workers; CTE institutions implementing the ACI model are that countervailing force (see [[cte-futures-aci-webinar]], [[applied-co-intelligence-model]], [[cte-and-ai]])
- **AI is reshaping the skill economy before the job economy**: Current AI disruption reorganizes which skills matter within jobs before eliminating jobs at scale; this positions CTE's immediate challenge as curriculum and credential design, not just labor market survival (see [[cte-futures-aci-webinar]], [[sinead-bovell-job-market]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **The attribution of the entry-level hiring decline to AI is contested**: The junior hiring decline is real (8–11pp below 2019 baselines across four countries), but Lambert & Schindler find that WFH exposure — not GenAI — is the robust predictor once both are estimated jointly; WFH and GenAI exposure correlate at 0.77 across occupations, making single-treatment designs unreliable; this is cause for optimism if true (WFH frictions are surmountable; AI-driven effects would require structural policy remedies) (see [[broken-ladder]], [[anthropic-labor-market-index]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **CTE occupations are doubly insulated from both post-pandemic shocks**: Electricians, construction workers, and other CTE-track occupations sit at the bottom of both WFH and GenAI exposure rankings — insulated from both remote-work organizational frictions and near-term AI task automation (see [[broken-ladder]], [[cte-and-ai]], [[bcg-ai-reshapes-jobs]])
- **The trade pivot is already happening — and CTE exposure is the mechanism**: In the AI capital itself, the class of 2026 is explicitly choosing trades as an AI hedge; over 95% of students in California's construction trades programs had never used tools before the class, confirming that CTE programs create the choice set rather than simply filling a pre-existing pipeline (see [[nyt-teens-trade-classes]], [[cte-and-ai]], [[cte-futures-aci-webinar]])
- **Oral defense renders AI detection moot**: "Build, analyze, defend" — students build with AI, analyze it critically, then defend orally — is the assessment scaffold that simultaneously increases cognition and eliminates the need for AI detection software; AI detection tools don't work and are creating legal liability for institutions that rely on them (see [[ai-unplugged-murgatroyd]], [[agentivism]], [[evidence-base-ai-k12]])
- **AI cultural bias is a structural equity problem, not just an accuracy problem**: AI systems trained on predominantly Western corpora produce outputs that misrepresent or erase indigenous and non-Western knowledge systems; data sovereignty — locally controlled, locally trained AI — is both a privacy solution and a cultural self-determination requirement (see [[ai-unplugged-murgatroyd]], [[ai-privacy-and-institutional-adoption]])
- **The productivity funnel: 740% velocity collapses to 20% shipped**: Demirer, Musolff & Yang (2026), 100,000+ GitHub developers across three AI tool generations, find a consistent productivity funnel — production velocity up 740%, review requests up 65%, shipped releases up only ~20%. Every human-dependent stage drains the gain. 95% of organizations see no measurable return (MIT NANDA); 80% AI project failure rate (RAND); 60% abandoned without data foundation (Gartner). The bottleneck is not AI capability — it is human review capacity (see [[hardman-ai-faster-slower]], [[building-ai-companions]], [[evidence-base-ai-k12]])
- **"Enter high in the chain" is the decisive AI deployment variable**: A modest AI tool aimed at high-value cognitive stages beats a powerful tool aimed at low-value production tasks, because fewer human-dependent stages remain to drain the gain. In education: AI for feedback, metacognitive calibration, and teacher coaching (the stages where cognition lives) outperforms AI for content generation. This principle unifies the productivity funnel, the learning-performance paradox, and the evidence on educator AI (see [[hardman-ai-faster-slower]], [[building-ai-companions]], [[evidence-base-ai-k12]])
- **Anthropomorphism compounds sycophancy — framing AI as "buddy/mentor" measurably reduces reviewer scrutiny**: Kropp et al. (2026) show that "AI colleague" framing produces sloppier review than "AI tool" framing; this operates independently of and compounds AI sycophancy. Together they produce maximum reduction in epistemic monitoring — the defense against cognitive debt, quality drag, and delusional spirals (see [[ai-sycophancy]], [[hardman-ai-faster-slower]], [[ai-delusional-spirals]])
- **AI diffusion is constrained by institutional change, not technology**: Narayanan & Kapoor (Knight Columbia) and Microsoft's own data (31.3% US adoption, Q1 2026) show broad transformation measured in decades, not years; the bottleneck is human, organizational, and institutional absorptive capacity — corroborating the productivity funnel finding that human stages drain AI's production gains (see [[brad-smith-ai-jobs-next-generation]], [[hardman-ai-faster-slower]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **The 2026 graduate generation is asserting the value of human work — both in career choices and in symbolic acts**: From trade enrollment in the Bay Area to commencement booing and "100% human" graduation jackets at Princeton, the class of 2026 is independently converging on the insulation thesis and the dignity-of-work argument — the same conclusions economists and researchers are reaching through formal analysis (see [[brad-smith-ai-jobs-next-generation]], [[nyt-teens-trade-classes]], [[transferable-skills]])
- **The "perfect storm" for 2026 graduates is multi-causal**: AI task automation + corporate headcount reductions to fund AI capex + COVID-era hiring overhang + geopolitical uncertainty. The junior-hiring decline cannot be attributed to any single mechanism; the policy remedies differ depending on which driver dominates (see [[brad-smith-ai-jobs-next-generation]], [[broken-ladder]], [[anthropic-labor-market-index]])
- **Zone 2 (scattered AI use) is worse than no AI — Zone 3 (strategic delegation) is dramatically better**: Wang & Zhang (2026), 912 students across three continents, find a U-shaped relationship between cognitive offloading and transformative learning. Scattered AI assists add coordination overhead without freeing capacity; committed delegation of entire task categories frees capacity for higher-order work and produces deep perspective-shifting. All the major negative studies (Kosmyna, Bastani, Fischer) measured Zone 2 conditions. Design — not the tool — determines which zone learners operate in (see [[hardman-cognitive-offloading-paradox]], [[cognitive-debt]], [[agentivism]])
- **Cognitive partnership (think with AI) and social anthropomorphism (trust AI like a friend) are opposite design moves**: Partnership orientation activates both vigilance and strategic delegation simultaneously; buddy/mentor framing reduces scrutiny. Institutions that frame AI as a cognitive tool requiring active evaluation produce Zone 3 conditions; those that frame AI as a helpful companion risk Zone 2 (see [[hardman-cognitive-offloading-paradox]], [[ai-sycophancy]], [[building-ai-companions]])
- **Automation predicts employment decline; augmentation does not**: Stanford DEL's Canaries Dashboard (4.6M workers, live ADP data, June 2026) finds that occupations with higher automation-type AI usage show clear negative correlation with early-career employment; occupations with higher augmentation usage show no relationship. It is not AI exposure per se but *how* AI is used that determines labor market outcomes (see [[stanford-del-ai-economic-indicators]], [[anthropic-labor-market-index]], [[labor-market-polarization]])
- **No decisive macroeconomic takeoff yet — but early-career divergence is real and compounding**: The Stanford DEL Takeoff Tracker (12 indicators, May 2026) finds TFP growth neutral and no decisive aggregate transformation; yet the Canaries Dashboard shows early-career AI-exposed workers contracting at −3.8%/yr vs. +2.0%/yr for least-exposed. The occupation-level signal is clear; the macro signal is not (see [[stanford-del-ai-economic-indicators]], [[labor-market-polarization]], [[brad-smith-ai-jobs-next-generation]])
- **Big Tech is explicitly investing in CTE trades training for AI infrastructure**: Meta's $115M America's Workforce Academy (free training, NCCER credentials, data center construction) and Zuckerberg's "hundreds of thousands of skilled tradespeople" statement are the highest-profile industry confirmations of the AI infrastructure → CTE demand claim; Level-Up predecessor attracted 35,000 applications in first week (see [[meta-workforce-academy]], [[cte-and-ai]], [[nber-data-centers-local-economies]])
