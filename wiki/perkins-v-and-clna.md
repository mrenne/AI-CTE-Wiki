# Perkins V and the Comprehensive Local Needs Assessment (CLNA)

**Summary**: Concept page for Perkins V, the main federal CTE funding law, and its Comprehensive Local Needs Assessment (CLNA) requirement. The CTE-sector sources in the wiki treat the CLNA as an existing, already-funded vehicle for keeping programs aligned with AI-driven labor-market change; this page sets out what the sources say the law requires, how they propose to use it, and how thin the evidence that it works is.

## What Perkins V is

The Carl D. Perkins Act has funded vocational education, and later CTE, since 1984. Each reauthorization shifted its emphasis (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf):

- **Perkins (1984)**: aimed to improve program quality, integrate academic and technical instruction, and tie programs more closely to industry.
- **Perkins IV (2006)**: introduced Programs of Study linking secondary and postsecondary education, raised academic rigor and outcome accountability, and encouraged industry-recognized credentials.
- **Perkins V (2018)**: described by the ACI report as "a new era of data-informed, equity-driven CTE policy." It introduced the CLNA and elevated work-based learning, industry-recognized credentials, career advisement, and dual enrollment.

**Funding.** Perkins V provides "over $1 billion annually" in state allocation grants, according to the ACI report and one of the four Fostering AI-Ready reports (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf; Fostering-AI-Ready-Cultivating-Resources-CTE-Pipelines.pdf). Two other reports in the same series say "billions of dollars" (source: Fostering-AI-Ready-Caring-for-Communities-CTE-Pipelines.pdf; Fostering-AI-Ready-Creating-and-Experiencing-CTE-Pipelines.pdf). The wording differs within one report series; the exact annual figure needs verification.

**The federal role.** The Fostering reports describe three functions: setting accountability targets for CTE providers, funding state allocation grants that supplement state money and reduce inequities, and connecting CTE to other laws, including WIOA, the Higher Education Act, IDEA, and ESSA (source: Fostering-AI-Ready-Caring-for-Communities-CTE-Pipelines.pdf). The Caring for Communities report argues, against proposed federal cuts, that this is "not a time for federal retreat" ([[fostering-ai-ready-caring-communities]]).

## What the CLNA requires

According to the sources, the CLNA requires local education agencies and postsecondary institutions to:

- use **labor-market data** to design and improve programs;
- use **performance indicators disaggregated by learner group**, so gaps for particular populations are visible (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf);
- **consult stakeholders, including local businesses**, and use what they learn to make funding decisions (source: Fostering-AI-Ready-Building-and-Moving-CTE-Pipelines.pdf).

The wiki's sources describe the CLNA only in these general terms. The statute's detailed requirements are not in any source the wiki holds.

## The argument: use the CLNA for AI alignment

Every CTE-sector source in the wiki makes the same recommendation: rather than wait for new AI legislation, use the CLNA.

- **The ACI report** argues that Perkins IV and V "readied (even if unwittingly) CTE for the present AI exigency" by building in the expectation that CTE evolves with work, and tells employers that the CLNA is "a great vehicle for launching [a] new era of public-private partnership" (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf; see [[applied-co-intelligence-report]]).
- **The ACI webinar** adds specifics. Panelists call the CLNA a vehicle to bridge the "chasm between providers and employers," in particular for finding out which occupation-specific AI tools (beyond general chatbots) employers actually use, so students can train on them. They also stress Perkins' special-populations emphasis as a guard against "stratifying CTE pipelines," and urge programs to build questions about AI use cases into standards review, program-of-study design, advisory structures, and the CLNA (source: Preparing CTE Learners for an AI-Driven Workforce_ Introducing An Applied Co-Intelligence Model.md; see [[cte-futures-aci-webinar]]).
- **All four Fostering AI-Ready reports** call the CLNA "a fantastic framework" for public-private partnerships on AI, and say the mandate "should be leveraged and improved to address any emergent AI skills mismatches in local economies" (source: Fostering-AI-Ready-Building-and-Moving-CTE-Pipelines.pdf). Note "and improved": the reports do not claim the CLNA is adequate as it stands.

[[responsive-by-design-career-clusters]]'s recommendation to rethink advisory-committee roles is a close parallel at the state-policy level, though that report does not name the CLNA (wiki inference, made on that page).

## Perkins money already funding AI capacity

Perkins funds are already being used for AI readiness in Tennessee:

- The **Tennessee Perkins READI AI Fellowship**, supported by the Tennessee Board of Regents, gives faculty $30,000+ to design and run AI-ready CTE projects with workforce partners (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf). CTE Futures, which produced the ACI report, is home to the fellowship, and the report's co-author Lauren Mason is a co-principal investigator, so it is the authors' own program (source: Preparing CTE Learners for an AI-Driven Workforce_ Introducing An Applied Co-Intelligence Model.md).
- A 2025 survey of all **37 Perkins CTE coordinators** in Tennessee's community and technical college system found over 90% agreed they "need support, guidance, and training" on AI; the top barrier was "a lack of faculty knowledge and expertise" (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf).

## Other federal levers

At the June 2026 Senate hearing, witnesses named **Title II and Title IV** (ESSA funding streams) as the levers for educator AI training. Erin Mote called fully funding them "critical," and Cindy Marten said "funding streams like Title IV" matter (source: Senate Hearing on Artificial Intelligence and K-12 Education.md; see [[senate-hearing-ai-k12-education]]). Perkins itself was not named in the transcript. Together these are the existing federal channels the sources point to: Perkins for CTE program alignment, Titles II and IV for K-12 educator training ([[ai-education-policy-and-legislation]]).

## Caveats

1. **No outcome evidence.** None of the wiki's sources evaluates whether CLNAs actually improve labor-market alignment or student outcomes. The recommendation rests on what the CLNA is designed to do. [[career-pathways-evidence-gap]] makes the wider point that states are building career pathways without the data systems to know whether they work (source: Career pathways are growing but evidence isn’t.md).
2. **Interested sources.** The recommendation comes entirely from the CTE field: CTE Futures (ACI report and webinar), and ERO with Advance CTE and ACTE (Fostering reports). No independent or critical source on the CLNA is in the wiki.
3. **"Compliance exercise" is unverified.** Two wiki pages ([[cte-futures-aci-webinar]], [[applied-co-intelligence-model]]) say practitioners treat the CLNA as a compliance exercise. That characterization does not appear in the webinar transcript and is marked as needing verification on those pages.

## Implications for CTE

These are the wiki's inferences, not claims made by the sources.

1. **The CLNA's labor-market step is where new AI evidence belongs.** The wiki holds occupation-level sources that could feed it: the [[stanford-del-ai-economic-indicators]] Canaries dashboard, the [[anthropic-labor-market-index]], and the [[entry-level-labor-market]] synthesis. The entry-level evidence suggests looking past total occupation headcount to hiring of new entrants, which can fall while an occupation grows ([[bcg-ai-reshapes-jobs]]).
2. **Disaggregation is the equity check.** Disaggregated indicators are where AI-driven stratification would show up, for example if AI-tool access or work-based-learning placements split along income lines.
3. **Employer consultation should ask about tools and tasks, not just job titles.** The webinar's point that employers use AI tools educators have never heard of suggests consultation should cover how work is changing inside occupations ([[cte-and-ai]], [[work-based-learning]]).
4. **Outcome data is the missing half.** A needs assessment says what to build; it cannot say whether it worked. Linking CLNA decisions to graduate employment and earnings data would address the evidence gap above.
