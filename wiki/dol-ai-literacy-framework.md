# DOL AI Literacy Framework (TEN 07-25)

**Summary**: The U.S. Department of Labor's AI Literacy Framework (Training and Employment Notice 07-25, February 2026), voluntary guidance addressed to the workforce and education systems, including state CTE directors. It defines AI literacy as baseline competencies to use and evaluate AI, mainly generative AI, responsibly, and sets out five content areas and seven delivery principles, without an evidence base on what works.

**Sources**: `raw/PDF/DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf`

**Last updated**: 2026-11-23

---

## About this source

Training and Employment Notice (TEN) 07-25, dated February 13, 2026, from DOL's Employment and Training Administration, signed by Assistant Secretary Henry Mack and Chief Innovation Officer Taylor Stockton. It is addressed to state workforce agencies and boards, American Job Centers, ETA grantees, community and tribal colleges, state apprenticeship agencies, Job Corps centers, state educational agencies, state education commissioners, and **state CTE directors** (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

**Reading it carefully:**
- **It is voluntary guidance.** DOL offers it "as a resource for program design" and says programs should keep the flexibility to choose their own approach (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).
- **It is a first version.** DOL calls it "a starting point" that will be updated as AI capabilities and labor markets change (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).
- **It is administration policy, not research.** It frames AI literacy in terms of the "America First worker agenda" and "reindustrialization," and cites no studies on what makes AI literacy training effective (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

## Policy lineage

The framework builds on a chain of 2025 federal actions (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf):

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| April 23, 2025 | Executive Order 14277, *Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth* |
| 2025 | *Winning the Race: America's AI Action Plan* (White House) |
| July 21–22, 2025 | Department of Education: Dear Colleague Letter on using federal grant funds for AI, and a proposed supplemental grantmaking priority on AI in education |
| 2025 | DOL's *America's Talent Strategy* |
| August 26, 2025 | TEGL 03-25, encouraging use of **WIOA funding** to help youth and adults develop AI skills, with preliminary AI literacy content areas |
| February 13, 2026 | TEN 07-25: this framework, expanding TEGL 03-25 |

DOL also points users to its Competency Model Clearinghouse, which it plans to update for digital and AI competencies (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

## The definition

DOL defines AI literacy as "a foundational set of competencies that enable individuals to use and evaluate AI technologies responsibly, with a primary focus on generative AI" (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

- **"Literacy" means a baseline.** It is the foundational level all workers and students should have; many roles will need "more advanced capabilities beyond this foundational level, such as managing and building AI systems" (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).
- **The terms are unsettled.** DOL notes that "AI proficiency" and "AI fluency" are sometimes used for higher levels and sometimes as synonyms, and that saying a job needs "AI literacy" is not enough on its own: employers may need to define the skills and depth each role requires (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

## Five foundational content areas

| Content area | Examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Understand AI principles** | Pattern recognition and probabilistic outputs; capabilities and modalities; training vs. inference; hallucinations and accuracy limits; human design and oversight |
| **2. Explore AI uses** | Productivity tools; information support; creative assistance; task-specific applications (code, transcription, data entry); decision support |
| **3. Direct AI effectively** | Contextual framing; prompting techniques; supplying relevant input data; iterating on outputs; avoiding vague prompts |
| **4. Evaluate AI outputs** | Verifying factual accuracy; assessing completeness and clarity; spotting gaps or logical errors; aligning with intent; applying human judgment |
| **5. Use AI responsibly** | Protecting sensitive information; following workplace policies; avoiding misuse; managing context-specific risk; maintaining accountability |

(source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf)

On evaluation, DOL's stated aim is that AI be "a support tool, but not a final authority" (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

## Seven delivery principles

| Principle | Examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Enable experiential learning** | Real-world tasks; prompt exercises including poor examples; live feedback; side-by-side comparison of AI and human work; progressive difficulty |
| **2. Embed learning in context** | Industry-specific examples; occupational tasks; employer systems and policies; integration into **Registered Apprenticeships, CTE curricula**, short-term credentials; cohort-specific pacing |
| **3. Build complementary human skills** | Critical thinking; creativity; communication; values-based decisions; domain expertise |
| **4. Address prerequisites** | Baseline readiness checks; digital literacy refreshers; device and broadband access; low-bandwidth materials |
| **5. Create pathways for continued learning** | Progression to "AI proficiency," including managing complex AI systems; builder and entrepreneurship routes; stackable models; occupation-specific progressions; pathways into AI careers |
| **6. Prepare enabling roles** | Train-the-trainer; manager upskilling; career counselors and mentors; peer champions; HR and L&D |
| **7. Design for agility** | Continuous content updates; feedback- and outcome-driven iteration; modular design |

(source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf)

Education and training providers may deliver AI literacy "as a standalone module, or embedding the content within existing learning pathways" (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

**What DOL asks for next.** DOL invites input on industry-specific guidance for incumbent worker training, Registered Apprenticeships, career and technical education, and K-20 programs, and plans to work with the companies that signed the White House pledge to provide free AI education resources (source: DOL AI Litreacy Framework.pdf).

## Implications for CTE

These are the wiki's inferences, not claims made by the source.

1. **CTE now has a federal reference definition.** Programs claiming to teach AI literacy can map their content to DOL's five areas, which state CTE directors received directly.
2. **The framework covers the floor only.** CTE's occupational focus lives in DOL's "continued learning" and "proficiency" territory, where the [[ai-mastery-continuum]]'s upper levels give more detail.
3. **WIOA is a funding route to check.** TEGL 03-25 lets workforce funds pay for AI skills training, which may support adult and incumbent-worker CTE.
4. **Teach evaluation inside the occupation.** DOL's emphasis on evaluation and domain expertise fits CTE, but the hard part, building students' ability to judge AI output, still lacks an evidence-based method.
5. **DOL is asking for CTE-specific input.** The invitation to propose industry-specific guidance for CTE and apprenticeships is an opening for CTE organizations.
