# Co-Intelligence

**Summary**: Ethan Mollick's concept of AI as a collaborative partner rather than a mere tool — the intellectual foundation that the Applied Co-Intelligence model adapts and extends for CTE learners.

**Sources**: `raw/PDF/Applied-Co-Intelligence–Preparing-Career-and-Technical-Education-Learners-for-an-AI-Driven-Workforce-FINAL.pdf`

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25

## Overview

Co-Intelligence is a framework introduced by Wharton professor Ethan Mollick in his 2024 book *Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI*. Mollick argues that generative AI is not merely a tool but a new kind of intelligence that humans should learn to work with as a collaborator, coach, or creative partner. The book provides a practical, hands-on guide for "living with AI" and demonstrating that the key to unlocking its power is to actively and experimentally integrate it into daily workflows to amplify human skills and creativity.

## Core Argument

Mollick's key insight is that the most effective AI users are those who lean in — who integrate AI into their daily work, discover its capabilities and limits through experimentation, and develop collaborative habits. Rather than treating AI as a search engine or a tool for specific tasks, co-intelligence means inviting AI into the full workflow as a thinking partner.

The framework encourages:
- Treating AI as a co-worker with complementary capabilities
- Using AI to amplify human strengths rather than outsource human judgment
- Experimenting with AI use across a wide range of tasks
- Maintaining human agency and oversight throughout

## Relevance to CTE: Gaps and Extensions

The [[applied-co-intelligence-report]] builds directly on Mollick's framework but identifies important gaps when applied to CTE contexts. Mollick's original model is primarily designed for **knowledge workers** — those doing abstract, cognitive, desk-based work. CTE learners are builders, caregivers, operators, and technicians in hands-on, occupation-specific environments (source: Applied-Co-Intelligence PDF).

Specific gaps:
1. The original framework overlooks the depth and specificity of technical skill development required in CTE (e.g., HVAC diagnostic procedures require mastery that general AI prompting cannot substitute for)
2. It does not address settings where AI integration is partial or context-dependent (a welding program may use AI for design simulations but not core execution)
3. It provides no operational framework for educators teaching AI within specific occupational contexts

These gaps motivate the [[applied-co-intelligence-model]] as a CTE-specific extension: Applied Co-Intelligence is co-intelligence grounded in skilled labor, where AI is not just a thought partner but a tool for doing.

## Contrast with Agentivism

Mollick's co-intelligence framework is primarily prescriptive and optimistic — a guide for how to work well with AI. [[agentivism]] provides the theoretical grounding for *why* certain modes of AI collaboration produce learning and others do not. Together they are complementary: co-intelligence describes the goal; agentivism explains the mechanism.
